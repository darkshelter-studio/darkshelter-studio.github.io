Learning to See the Light

Before thinking about cameras and settings, start by noticing the light around you.

A photograph begins with light. It might come through a window, fall across a building or disappear behind a passing cloud. Learning to notice these small changes is one of the simplest ways to become more comfortable with photography.

Think about the difference between photographing a subject in direct sunlight and photographing it on an overcast day. In strong sunlight, shadows are often sharp and clearly defined. On a cloudy day, the light becomes softer, spreading more evenly across the scene.

The direction of the light matters too. Light coming from the side can reveal the texture of a wall or the details in someone’s face. Light coming from behind a subject can create silhouettes and interesting outlines.

With film photography, these observations become especially important. Film needs light to record an image, and the amount available influences how you expose your photograph.

Try returning to the same place at different times of day. Notice how the shadows move, how colours change and how the same scene can feel completely different.

You don’t need a camera to begin learning about light. Sometimes it’s enough to stop and look.
