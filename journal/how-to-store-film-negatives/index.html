Looking After Your Film Negatives

The photographs may be printed, but the negatives are worth keeping.

Once a roll of film has been developed, you have something worth looking after. The negatives contain the original images recorded by your camera, and they can be used to make new prints or scans whenever you need them.

Film is delicate, particularly once it has been developed. Dust, fingerprints and scratches can affect the image, so it’s best to handle negatives by their edges rather than touching the surface.

For storage, use archival-quality negative sleeves designed for photographic film. They help protect the strips from dust and unnecessary handling. Keep them somewhere cool, dry and away from direct sunlight or sudden changes in temperature and humidity.

It’s also worth labelling each roll. A simple note with the date, camera and type of film can make a big difference when you’re looking for a particular photograph later.

Even if you’ve already scanned your favourite frames or made prints, don’t throw the negatives away. They are the original material, and you may want to return to them years from now.

A photograph can be printed more than once, but looking after the negative means keeping the original image safe.
