Choose Your Film Format, Then Slow Down

35mm, medium format or large format: three different ways of making a photograph.

Not all film cameras use the same size of film. Some use small rolls that let you take several photographs, while others use larger film or even individual sheets, making each photograph a more deliberate process.

35mm film is the most familiar starting point. It comes in small rolls, usually containing 24 or 36 exposures. The cameras are often compact, easy to carry and convenient for everyday photography.

120 film, also known as medium format, is wider than 35mm film and produces a much larger negative. Depending on the camera, photographs can be square or rectangular. Common frame sizes include 6×4.5, 6×6 and 6×7 cm. The larger negative offers more room for detail, while each roll produces fewer photographs.

Large format works differently again. Instead of a roll, the camera uses individual sheets of film, with each sheet making one photograph. The camera is usually mounted on a tripod, and the image is composed on a glass screen before the photograph is taken. The process takes more time, but gives you greater control over how the image is made.

Each format has its own character, advantages and way of working. There is no single right place to begin. The best way to understand the difference is to see the cameras, handle them and discover which process interests you.
