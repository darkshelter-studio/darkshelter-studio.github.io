What Happens to Your Film After You Finish Shooting?

From the camera to the negatives: a little look at what happens when your film comes to the studio.

When you finish a roll of film, the photographs are still hidden. There is no screen to look at and no way to know exactly what you’ve captured. The images are recorded on a light-sensitive strip inside your camera, waiting to be developed.

Developing is the process that makes these images visible. The film is first loaded into a developing tank, where it can be treated with chemicals without being exposed to light. It then goes through a series of stages, beginning with the developer, which brings out the images recorded on the film.

After fixing and washing, the film can finally be taken out and seen. What was once a blank-looking strip is now filled with photographs, appearing as a series of negatives.

The negatives are the original versions of your photographs. They can be scanned, printed in the darkroom or carefully stored for another day.

It’s always a lovely moment to see a roll for the first time. Some frames turn out just as you imagined, while others surprise you completely.
