From Negative to Photograph

How an image on a small piece of film becomes something you can hold in your hands.

A developed negative is not quite the photograph you remember taking. It is a small piece of film where the light and dark areas of the original scene appear reversed. To turn it into a print, we need to bring it into the darkroom.

The negative is placed inside a photographic enlarger, a piece of equipment that projects light through the film and onto light-sensitive photographic paper. A lens focuses the image, allowing us to make it larger than the original negative.

At first, the paper looks completely blank. After exposure, it is placed into a tray of developer. Slowly, an image begins to appear. Light areas, dark shadows and familiar details start to take shape.

Once the print has been developed, fixed and washed, the photograph can be brought out of the darkroom.

What makes this process interesting is that the negative doesn’t determine exactly what the final print will look like. You can make decisions about exposure, contrast and the way the image is presented.

One negative can become several prints, each a little different.

The photograph isn’t simply copied from the film. It is made, one decision at a time.

You can learn this process hands-on in our darkroom workshop.
