Choosing Your First Roll of Film

Before you buy a roll, a few things worth knowing about film speed, colour and light.

Standing in front of a shelf of film for the first time can be a little confusing. Different names, numbers and boxes all promise something slightly different. But choosing your first roll doesn’t need to be complicated.

One of the first things to look at is the ISO number. This tells you how sensitive the film is to light. A lower number, such as ISO 100, is generally suited to brighter conditions. ISO 400 is more flexible for everyday photography, while ISO 800 can be useful when working in lower light.

You’ll also need to decide whether you want to shoot colour or black and white. Colour film records the different colours in a scene, while black-and-white film turns them into shades of grey, with their own relationships between highlights and shadows.

Another thing to check is the format. Most compact film cameras use 35mm film, while medium-format cameras generally use 120 film. The two are not interchangeable, so it’s worth checking your camera before buying.

If you’re just starting out, ISO 400 is a good place to begin. Choose a film that suits your camera, take it outside and give yourself time to get to know it.

You don’t need to try everything at once. One roll is enough to start learning what you like.
